02
Strategic Vision

Not a new idea.
A better execution.

Shared commutes have existed for decades — shared rickshaws in Mumbai, colectivos in Latin America, matatus in Kenya. Billions of people already trust this model. The problem isn't demand. It's coordination, safety, and reliability.

Uber has the GPS, the payment rails, the driver network, and the brand trust. The only thing missing is a product built specifically for the daily commute — pre-aggregated demand, guaranteed departure times, and door-to-door service.

Take what billions already do informally and make it something they can rely on every morning.

Global Scalability
Works in any city with concentrated employment zones and residential belts.
Daily Utility
Transform Uber from an occasional service to a daily commute habit worth keeping.
Rider Trust
Platform handles payments, safety, and fairness — no trust needed between strangers.
03
How It Works

Grouping logic meets
distance-based pricing.

The product has two moving parts — matching riders efficiently, and pricing them fairly. Both need to work before either one matters.

Grouping Logic
How riders get matched
  • Zone-based matching with door-to-door delivery
  • Max 5 passengers per vehicle (sedan or SUV)
  • All drop points within ~3 km radius
  • Compatible arrival time windows required
  • Gender preference options available
  • Stable group recognition builds over time
  • Rotating drop order ensures fairness
Pricing Model
Distance-based, per rider
  • Individual distance-based fares — no splitting
  • Swords (closest): €8 per rider
  • Malahide (mid-distance): €10 per rider
  • Balbriggan (farthest): €14 per rider
  • Driver earns combined total (~€35–40 per trip)
  • Weekly and monthly subscription passes
  • 60–70% savings vs. solo ride for each rider

Riders pay individually based on their distance, not an equal split. Fairness is what makes the group willing to ride together every single day.

05
Assembly Point

The morning pickup
starts at a café.

Most shared ride schemes fail at the pickup. Standing on a dark street corner at 6am waiting for strangers isn't something people do twice.

Uber Commute uses a nearby café or restaurant as the assembly point — not a bus stop, not a street corner. Riders get shelter, seating, and a coffee while they wait. The venue gets guaranteed daily foot traffic from the same people every morning.

Safety improves. The habit forms. The partnership pays for itself.

Comfort
Shelter, seating, washrooms, and chai while you wait — not a windswept bus stop.
Safety
Well-lit staffed venue — especially important for women commuting at early hours.
Revenue
Partner cafés earn from guaranteed daily foot traffic — a partnership that makes them want to participate.
Habit Loop
Same place, same people, same coffee every morning. Hard to break once it's set.

07
Launch Strategy

Start small.
Scale fast.

The launch is deliberately constrained — prove the model works on three corridors before touching the algorithm. Manual ops first, automation second.

Months 1–3
Proof of Concept
3 corridors in Mumbai, employer partnerships, manual matching ops, 10 rides/day target per corridor.
Months 4–8
Algorithm & Scale
Automated matching, 15 corridors across Mumbai and Delhi, subscription model launch.
Months 9–14
Multi-City
Bangalore, Hyderabad + 1 international market, automated corridor detection from ride data.
Months 15–24
Platform Maturity
10+ cities globally, university corridors, transit integration, dynamic pricing by corridor demand.
